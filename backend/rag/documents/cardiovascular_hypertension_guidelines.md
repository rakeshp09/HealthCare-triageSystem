# Clinical Protocol: Acute Chest Pain, Hypertensive Urgency & Cardiovascular Triage

## 1. Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS) Red Flags
Every patient presenting with chest pain, chest heaviness, epigastric tightness, or jaw/left arm radiation must undergo triage screening within 10 minutes of arrival.
- **Classic Red Flags**:
  - Crushing retrosternal chest pain > 20 minutes duration.
  - Pain radiating to neck, lower jaw, epigastrium, or left shoulder/arm.
  - Associated diaphoresis (cold sweats), nausea, syncope, or acute dyspnea.
  - Atypical presentations in diabetics, elderly, and female patients (isolated breathlessness, unexplained fatigue, or acute delirium).

## 2. Emergency Actions for Suspected ACS
1. Immediate 12-lead Electrocardiogram (ECG) performed and interpreted within 10 minutes.
2. Continuous Lead-II cardiac telemetry monitoring.
3. Aspirin 300mg chewed immediately (unless documented absolute anaphylaxis).
4. Establish peripheral intravenous access.
5. If STEMI (ST-segment elevation >= 1mm in two contiguous leads), immediately activate emergency catheterization referral or thrombolysis pathway.

## 3. Hypertensive Urgency vs. Hypertensive Emergency
- **Hypertensive Urgency**:
  - Systolic BP >= 180 mmHg and/or Diastolic BP >= 110 mmHg **without** acute target organ damage.
  - Management: Oral antihypertensive adjustment (Amlodipine, Telmisartan, or Clonidine); outpatient review within 24-48 hours.
- **Hypertensive Emergency**:
  - Systolic BP >= 180 mmHg or Diastolic >= 120 mmHg **with** evidence of acute target organ failure:
    - Neurologic: Encephalopathy, intracranial hemorrhage, acute ischemic stroke.
    - Cardiac: Acute pulmonary edema, myocardial infarction, aortic dissection.
    - Renal: Acute kidney injury with oliguria.
    - Ocular: Grade III/IV retinopathy (papilledema, flame hemorrhages).
  - Priority: `CRITICAL`. Immediate referral to intensive care unit with parenteral titratable IV antihypertensives (Labetalol, Nitroglycerin, or Nicardipine).
