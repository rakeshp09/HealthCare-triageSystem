# Clinical Protocol: Maternal Obstetric & Pediatric Triage Red Flags

## 1. Obstetric Emergency Red Flags
Any pregnant or postpartum patient presenting with any of the following requires immediate priority escalation:
- **Severe Preeclampsia / Eclampsia Warning Signs**:
  - Systolic BP >= 160 mmHg or Diastolic BP >= 110 mmHg.
  - Intractable frontal headache unresponsive to analgesics.
  - Visual disturbances (scotomata, blurring, flashing lights).
  - Severe epigastric or right upper quadrant abdominal pain.
  - Action: Administer Magnesium Sulfate seizure prophylaxis protocol, control blood pressure, and dispatch emergency obstetric transfer.
- **Antepartum / Postpartum Hemorrhage**:
  - Vaginal bleeding > 500 mL, soaking pads rapidly, signs of hypovolemic shock (pallor, pulse > 110, BP < 90/60).
- **Premature Rupture of Membranes & Cord Prolapse**:
  - Immediate knee-chest positioning and emergent transport.

## 2. Pediatric Emergency Assessment Triangle (PAT)
Assess three key components without touching the child first:
1. **Appearance**: Tone, interactiveness, consolability, look/gaze, speech/cry (TICLS mnemonic).
2. **Work of Breathing**: Grunting, nasal flaring, retractions, stridor, wheezing.
3. **Circulation to Skin**: Pallor, mottling, peripheral cyanosis.

- If any 1 component abnormal: Sick child requiring immediate doctor review.
- If >= 2 components abnormal: Severe physiological failure (respiratory failure or decompensated shock); immediate life-saving intervention.
