# Clinical Protocol: Acute Dyspnea, Asthma, and Respiratory Triage Guidelines

## 1. Clinical Presentation & Red Flags
Acute shortness of breath (dyspnea) requires rapid assessment of airway patency, work of breathing, and gas exchange.
- **Red Flags**:
  - Inability to speak in full sentences (broken speech).
  - Accessory muscle use, intercostal indrawing, or tracheal tug.
  - Cyanosis (perioral or nail beds).
  - Silent chest on auscultation (severe airflow limitation).
  - SpO2 < 92% on room air.

## 2. Oxygen Saturation (SpO2) Target Protocol
- **General Adult Target**: 94% - 98%.
- **Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) Target**: 88% - 92% (to prevent hypercapnic respiratory depression).
- **Critical Action**: If SpO2 < 92% on room air in non-COPD patient, initiate high-flow nasal cannula or mask oxygen at 4-6 L/min and tag priority as CRITICAL.

## 3. Asthma & Bronchospasm Exacerbation
- **Mild to Moderate**: Wheezing, tachypnea (RR 20-25), SpO2 92-95%. Nebulize Salbutamol 2.5mg + Ipratropium 0.5mg.
- **Severe**: Stridor, RR > 28, HR > 110, SpO2 < 92%. Initiate back-to-back nebulization, oral/IV corticosteroids (Prednisolone 40-50mg), and prepare ICU / specialist handoff.

## 4. Pneumonia & Pulmonary Infiltrates
- Triage criteria (CURB-65 criteria):
  - **C**: Confusion / altered mental status
  - **U**: Blood urea nitrogen > 19 mg/dL
  - **R**: Respiratory rate >= 30/min
  - **B**: Systolic BP < 90 mmHg or Diastolic <= 60 mmHg
  - **65**: Age >= 65 years
  - Score >= 2 requires immediate hospital admission; score >= 3 requires intensive care unit evaluation.
